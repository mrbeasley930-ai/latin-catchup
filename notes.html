Year 10 Latin · Half-Term Catch-Up

How to keep your notes

Over the ten days you will build one file of notes on your computer. It replaces the exercise-book pages the class copied. By the end of the fortnight it is your revision file for the whole of this grammar, and it comes back to school with you. Set it up before Day 1. It takes about ten minutes.

1 · One file, in one place

Use Word through your school Microsoft account, so the file lives on OneDrive and opens on any computer (school laptop, home computer, the browser version of Word). If you only have Google at home, Google Docs does the same job. What matters is that there is one file and it is saved somewhere that follows you, not a file on the desktop of one computer.

  1. Open Word (or Google Docs) and make a new blank document.
  2. Name it Latin Catch-Up Notes. In Word online the name is at the top of the window; click it and type. On the desktop app, save it straight to OneDrive with that name.
  3. On the first line, type Latin Catch-Up Notes — Year 10 — October half term and make it a title (the Title style under Home, or just bold and bigger).
  4. Press Enter twice. That is it. Each day you add a new section underneath.
Word and Google Docs both save by themselves every few seconds when the file is on OneDrive or Google Drive. You will see ‘Saved’ at the top. If you ever see ‘Not saved’ or a warning, press Ctrl + S (Mac: ⌘ + S).

2 · What each day’s section looks like

Every day the workbook shows you a page of notes and then a list headed Type into your notes file. Work down that list in order. Each section has the same four parts:

Day 1 — Comparatives — Mon 19 Oct

1. Make it: genitive stem + -ior.

adjectivegenitivestemcomparativemeaning
laetuslaetilaet-laetiorhappier
fortisfortisfort-fortiorbraver

A comparative is a 3rd-declension word, like miles: fortior, fortiorem, fortiores.

genitive stem + -ior · declines like miles · quam = than, same case both sides

servus stultior est quam dominus. = The slave is more stupid than the master.

  1. The heading. Day number, the topic and the date, as a heading (choose Heading 1 under Home). Using a real heading style matters: Word then lists every day in the Navigation pane (View → Navigation Pane; in Google Docs, View → Show outline) so you can jump between days.
  2. The table. Insert → Table, then pick the number of columns and rows the page shows. Press Tab to move to the next cell; Tab in the last cell adds a new row. Type the Latin in italics (Ctrl + I) if you can; do not worry if you forget.
  3. The boxed rule, in bold (Ctrl + B). This is the one line to read back when you revise.
  4. One example sentence with its English underneath.

Type it; do not copy and paste. The notes page in the workbook will not let you copy from it, on purpose. Typing a table slowly is slower than copying it, and that is the point: your hands go through every ending.

3 · Put the two windows side by side

You need the workbook and the notes file on the screen at the same time, so you are not flipping back and forth.

Windows. Click the workbook window, press ⊞ Win + ←. Click the Word window, press ⊞ Win + →. They now fill half the screen each.
Mac. Hold the mouse on the green button at the top left of the workbook window and choose Tile Window to Left of Screen; then click the Word window to fill the right.

On a small laptop screen you can instead keep them as two browser tabs (Word online opens in a tab) and switch with Ctrl + Tab. Side by side is better if the screen allows it.

4 · The ten-minute routine, every day

  1. Open the notes file first, scroll to the bottom, and type the new day’s heading.
  2. Open the workbook, sign in (class code Y10, your name), go to the day. Read the notes page twice.
  3. Type the list into the notes file, in order. Then tick I have typed today’s notes in the workbook.
  4. Do the questions. Keep the notes file open on the other half of the screen and look at it whenever you need the table — that is what it is for.
  5. When the day is finished, read your boxed rule aloud once, and close both windows.

5 · Day 9 and Day 10

Day 9 asks for a plan of the dining room. Draw it on paper, large, with the nine numbers, photograph it on a phone and put the photo into the notes file (Insert → Pictures, or drag it in). A 3-by-3 table in Word also works if you would rather type it: the middle couch along the top row, the two side couches down the sides, the table in the centre cell, and ‘OPEN SIDE’ in the bottom middle.

Day 10 is the vocabulary list, 51 words. Make it a two-column table, Latin on the left, English on the right, in two parts headed Set A and Set B. To learn it afterwards, cover the right-hand column with a piece of paper and work down: say the meaning, slide the paper down, tick or cross in the margin. Do the crosses again the next day.

6 · After half term

Bring the file to the first Latin lesson back: open it on your laptop or share it with Mr Beasley on Teams. He will read it, and the two of you will go through anything from the fortnight that has not settled. From then on it is your revision file for comparatives, superlatives, hic and ille and the two dinner-party lessons, so keep it.
